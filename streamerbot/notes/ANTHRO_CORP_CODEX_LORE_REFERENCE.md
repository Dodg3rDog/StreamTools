# Anthro-Corp Lore & Widget Reference for Codex

> Purpose: This file gives Codex enough Anthro-Corp context to build streaming widgets, overlays, terminal UI, chat interactions, and themed assets without needing the full lore bible open at all times.
>
> Use this as a **creative and technical style guide**, not as a complete canon bible. When uncertain, preserve flexibility and avoid inventing major canon events as final truth.

---

## 1. Project Identity

**Anthro-Corp** is a sci-fi / mystery / thriller / office-drama setting centered on a powerful corporation involved in advanced genetics, anthropomorphic transformation, body modification, interdimensional research, and workplace/corporate secrecy.

For streaming tools and widgets, Anthro-Corp should feel like:

- A polished but suspicious megacorp.
- Corporate onboarding meets secret research facility.
- Retro terminal UI evolving into futuristic biotech dashboard UI.
- Mainframe, HR portal, research lab, employee badge, internal memo, classified system log.
- Professional on the surface; unsettling, playful, and conspiratorial underneath.

The stream/community side can present viewers as employees, test subjects, interns, contractors, or members of internal divisions. Widgets should feel like they are part of an in-universe corporate system rather than generic overlays.

---

## 2. Core Setting Summary

Anthro-Corp exists in an alternate Earth timeline where cosmetic gene alteration and body modification became increasingly common in the late 2000s. Over decades, the technology advanced from cosmetic modification into full-body transformation.

Anthro-Corp became the industry leader in this field and eventually announced a gene-editing process capable of creating entirely new humanoid anthro species.

Important setting beats:

- **Late 2000s:** Cosmetic gene alteration becomes popular and heavily funded.
- **2073:** Full body transformation technology is becoming viable. Anthro-Corp is the leading company.
- **2090:** Body modification becomes more mainstream as side effects decrease.
- **2096:** Anthro-Corp announces a gene-editing process promising complete body transition and the creation of a new humanoid species.
- **2098:** Human trials begin. Controversy intensifies, especially from anti-modification and anti-anthro groups.
- **2100:** Tensions rise around whether new “Anthros” fit into society. Anti-anthro groups sometimes call them “Beasts.”
- **2105:** Pro-anthro and anti-anthro groups clash after discriminatory local business practices. Deaths occur on both sides.
- **2107:** Courts rule that Anthros are protected under anti-discrimination law. Anthro and Anthro/Human marriages are legally recognized.
- **2108:** Anthro-Corp announces more available species and a fertility/procreation improvement for transitioned Anthros.
- **2109:** The first official naturally conceived Anthro is born to a rabbit couple. Mixed-species couples can also conceive under certain conditions.

## 3. Rift / Interdimensional Arc

The larger Anthro-Corp arc also involves **rift phenomena** and interdimensional research.

Known creative direction:

- A rift is discovered in the 1960s in the Anthro-Corp universe.
- Anthro-Corp has roots in government contracting and quarantine-zone research.
- The company may have originally formed or expanded as a front/contractor to manage a quarantined rift site.
- Anthro-Corp’s universe and the user/viewer universe do not necessarily share the same flow of time.
- Anthro-Corp’s universe can move faster while the viewer/user universe moves slower.
- Terminals in the viewer’s world may intercept Anthro-Corp transmissions that Anthro-Corp itself never receives.
- A long-term arc may involve syncing timelines later.

For widgets, this means terminal messages, alerts, and overlays can act like:

- Intercepted internal transmissions.
- Corrupted cross-dimensional packets.
- Employee records leaking across timelines.
- HR systems receiving impossible viewer data.
- Research systems identifying chatters as anomalous entities.

Do **not** lock in new rift rules unless explicitly requested. Keep the rift mystery flexible.

## 4. Anthros, Species, and Transformation Rules

Anthros are transformed or naturally born humanoid animal people created through Anthro-Corp’s gene-editing/body-transition technology.

Known species category structure includes:

- Aquatic
- Avian
- Bovine
- Canine
- Equine
- Feline
- Human
- Reptilian

Breeding/procreation notes:

- Breeding is possible if both parents are the same species.
- Breeding may be possible if both parents have similar traits.
- Breeding may be possible if both parents share a species lineage or compatible species basis.
- By 2109, mixed couples can conceive offspring under certain conditions.

Important tone rule: In technical widgets, do not over-explain biological details. Keep it as corporate terminology, species metadata, compatibility classes, or HR/research classifications.

Good UI terms:

- Species Profile
- Phenotype Class
- Genetic Template
- Compatibility Index
- Transition Status
- Employee Species Code
- Division Assignment
- Clearance Level
- Anomaly Marker

Avoid making hard biological canon unless directly specified.

## 9. Terminal UI Direction

Anthro-Corp has an interactive terminal concept.

Terminal behavior and style:

- Begins as text-only.
- No mouse UI at first.
- Commands include things like `HELP`, `LOGIN`, and `REGISTER`.
- Registration may ask for display name, division, and species.
- Registration generates an employee ID and access key.
- Accounts are intended eventually; do not rely purely on cookies for major progress.
- Later UI upgrades may unlock more HR prompts, richer visual systems, email verification, or color terminal features.
- Avoid exposing secret commands in obvious front-end code when possible.

Terminal aesthetic:

- Retro mainframe.
- Green-screen or amber phosphor early on.
- Gradually upgrades into cleaner corporate biotech dashboard visuals.
- ASCII logos and classified headers are welcome.
- Copy should feel like system logs, not normal website marketing.

### Terminal Copy Examples

```text
ANTHRO-CORP INTERNAL ACCESS NODE
SIGNAL ORIGIN: UNVERIFIED
RIFT LATENCY: 04.77 YEARS
STATUS: PARTIAL SYNC
```

```text
> REGISTER
DISPLAY NAME ACCEPTED
DIVISION ASSIGNMENT: PENDING
SPECIES TEMPLATE: UNVERIFIED
EMPLOYEE RECORD CREATED
```

```text
WARNING: This terminal is receiving packets from an unsynchronized timeline.
Do not report this incident to standard IT.
```

## 10. Widget Design Language

Widgets should feel like Anthro-Corp internal tools, not generic stream alerts.

### Visual Motifs

- Corporate blue accents.
- Dark interface panels.
- Terminal green, amber, or cyan highlights.
- Scanlines, subtle CRT effects, data glitches.
- Badge frames, ID cards, access panels.
- Lab readouts, gauges, meters, status indicators.
- Warning labels, clearance tags, classified stamps.
- Modular panels with rounded or beveled corners.
- Futuristic but readable typography.

### Useful UI Components

- Employee card
- Division badge
- Species marker
- Clearance chip
- Signal strength meter
- Rift stability gauge
- Mutation/progression bar
- Compliance status indicator
- Corporate ticker
- Classified memo popup
- Scan result panel
- Login shell
- Diagnostic console

### Accessibility / Stream Readability

For OBS overlays:

- Text must be readable at streaming resolution.
- Avoid tiny terminal text unless it is decorative.
- Provide compact and full-size modes when useful.
- Important information should have strong contrast.
- Avoid overly dense paragraphs in overlays.
- Animations should be stylish but not prevent reading.
- Tickers can be separate from compact widgets if space is limited.

## 11. Widget Mode Pattern

Many Anthro-Corp widgets should support multiple display modes.

Recommended modes:

- `full` — large display, detailed panels, lore text, gauges, ticker.
- `compact` — smaller widget, core status only, minimal text.
- `mini` — tiny overlay, status light, value, short label.
- `terminal` — text-heavy retro version.
- `classified` — more dramatic warning/containment styling.
- `club` — after-hours Night Howler styling.

For implementation, prefer CSS classes or query params:

```text
?mode=full
?mode=compact
?mode=mini
?theme=terminal
?theme=classified
```

## 12. StreamTools / Local Server Context

The user builds stream widgets using local web-server infrastructure for OBS browser sources.

Common architecture expectations:

- Node / Express server.
- Local network hosting.
- Widgets served as HTML/CSS/JS.
- OBS browser sources load widget URLs.
- Streamer.bot may call API endpoints.
- WebSocket/socket.io events may update overlays live.
- Assets should be organized by widget where possible.
- Static files should normally be served from a public/static folder.
- Avoid hardcoding one-off paths if a clean widget folder structure can be used.

Typical local server style:

```text
server/
  server.js
  routes/
  middleware/
  widgets/
    pishock-status/
      index.html
      widget.css
      widget.js
      assets/
```

Or:

```text
public/
  widgets/
    anthro-corp-status/
      index.html
      styles.css
      app.js
```

When writing code for these widgets, prioritize:

- Clean separation of server routes and front-end widget files.
- Environment variables for tokens/secrets.
- No secrets committed to the repo.
- Easy OBS URLs.
- Debug mode toggles.
- Health endpoints.
- Graceful fallback if APIs are unavailable.
- Preloading assets where practical.

## 20. What Codex Should Not Assume

Do not assume:

- Anthro-Corp is purely evil. It should feel powerful, secretive, and morally complicated.
- Every widget needs heavy lore exposition.
- The current lore timeline is final.
- All species rules are fully defined.
- The rift mechanics are fully explained.
- Viewer identities are permanent unless the app stores them intentionally.
- Adult/fetish comic themes should appear in public overlays by default.

When uncertain, create flexible systems with configurable labels and theme modes.

---

## 21. Best Default Implementation Approach

When Codex builds or edits an Anthro-Corp widget, it should:

1. Keep the widget readable in OBS.
2. Use Anthro-Corp terminology in labels and alerts.
3. Keep lore references modular/configurable.
4. Avoid hardcoding secrets.
5. Add debug-friendly fallbacks.
6. Use local assets when available.
7. Keep code organized by widget.
8. Prefer clean APIs and socket events over hacky DOM-only behavior.
9. Use `full`, `compact`, and `mini` modes when the widget may appear in multiple stream layouts.
10. Leave room for future lore expansion.
